\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We answer five questions. \textbf{Q1} Does simulation transfer to objects with no real data, and for which task? \textbf{Q2} How does the benefit scale with the real budget $\Kreal$ for each renderer? \textbf{Q3} Does the recipe transfer to a second sensor? \textbf{Q4} What happens across capture sessions? \textbf{Q5} How much does leakage inflate the benefit, can fidelity be measured without training, and which appearance factor limits it? All numbers are real-frame errors of the downstream model after 150 epochs; depth MSE in $\mathrm{mm}^2$, contact MAE in mm, normals and rotation in degrees.

\noindent\textbf{Pose.} Without any data of the three objects the pose head is at chance ($64.7^\circ$). Simulated contacts of the three meshes bring it to $6.8^\circ$ with the physical renderer---calibrated from one no-contact frame and never shown a real contact---and to $5.7^\circ$ with the strict learned renderer; the leaky renderer ($4.6^\circ$) buys little, and the geometry control (same renderer, meshes removed) stays at $50.6^\circ$. Pose transfer is geometric coverage; any renderer that preserves the contact silhouette suffices.

\noindent\textbf{Rotation coverage.} How much of the pose space must simulation cover? Restricting simulation to one real rotation per object leaves $10.3^\circ$, two rotations $7.5^\circ$, all twelve $6.8^\circ$ (Blender rows of Table~\ref{tab:loo}); rendering the learned renderer's inputs after rotating the depth map (rotate-then-render) gives the best leakage-free pose, $4.4^\circ$. Coverage of the rotation range, not the number of synthetic frames, is the operative variable.

\noindent\textbf{Depth and normals.} Contact MAE on the unseen objects is $0.36\,$mm for real-only training, three times the seen-object value; the plain renderers reduce it by at most $4\%$ and the geometry control by almost as much ($0.340$), so this is regularization by extra frames, not object coverage. The one leakage-free renderer that helps is the background-conditioned GAN-LOO: depth MSE $-13\%$, contact MAE $0.323$, IoU $0.633$, for one background frame per session; it remains far from the leaky reference ($-30\%$), which has seen real contacts of the objects. Normal error follows contact MAE. Peak-depth error is $0.05$--$0.08\,$mm for every row: the models estimate \emph{how deep} correctly and err in \emph{where} the contact lies.

Table~\ref{tab:kshot} and Fig.~\ref{fig:kshot} vary the real budget (three seeds at $\Kreal\in\{25,100\}$, 50 epochs). Three regimes appear.

\noindent\textbf{Zero-shot.} Simulation alone transfers poorly for dense geometry (contact MAE $0.41\,$mm vs.\ $0.11$ with the full real set); the leaky renderer halves this but has seen $4{,}197$ real pairs.

\noindent\textbf{Few-shot ($\Kreal\le25$).} Neither leakage-free renderer improves dense error. At $\Kreal=25$ GAN-25 lowers depth MSE by $3\%$ ($0.0536\pm0.0013$ vs.\ $0.0551\pm0.0010$ over seeds) but not contact MAE or IoU; the physical renderer does the same. With $\Kreal\le25$ the real training loss reaches $10^{-4}$ within ten epochs while validation error is flat from epoch five: the model memorizes the few real frames, and synthetic frames with a different contact appearance do not regularize that memorization. Simulation does help pose at $\Kreal=10$ ($9.6^\circ\!\to\!5.2$--$5.9^\circ$), as in Sec.~\ref{sec:loo}.

\noindent\textbf{Moderate budget ($\Kreal\ge50$).} The learned renderer now helps: at $\Kreal=50$ depth MSE $0.0482\!\to\!0.0434$ ($-10\%$), contact MAE $0.283\!\to\!0.260$, IoU $0.69\!\to\!0.72$; at $\Kreal=100$ depth MSE $0.0369\!\to\!0.0311$ ($-16\%$; $0.0366\!\to\!0.0299$ over seeds, $-18\%$), contact MAE $0.235\!\to\!0.210$, IoU $0.74\!\to\!0.77$, and normal error improves in step. The physical renderer gives $-1$ to $-5\%$ MSE. The learned renderer thus needs $\approx\!50$ labeled contacts per object before its data is worth more than the frames it was trained on, and at every $\Kreal\ge50$ simulation \emph{worsens} rotation by $0.2$--$1.3^\circ$.

Sensor B has a marker array, a different LED layout and a smaller field of view (Table~\ref{tab:sensorb}). \emph{Zero-shot}, sensor-A models recover contact only partially (IoU $0.28$ for the all-real model, $0.37$ for the simulation-only model: the model trained on the most sensor-A real data transfers worst) and rotation not at all (residual $\approx11.5^\circ$ for every model, the value a constant predictor attains on the $\pm22.5^\circ$ label range). \emph{With sensor-B real pairs}, the $\Kreal$-shot picture of Sec.~\ref{sec:kshot} reappears: a learned renderer trained on the sensor's own $\Kreal=25$ pairs lowers contact MAE from $0.139$ to $0.100\,$mm ($-29\%$), normal error from $17.2^\circ$ to $14.6^\circ$ and raises IoU from $0.82$ to $0.85$; at $\Kreal=10$ the gain is $6\%$, at $\Kreal=100$ real data alone is already at $0.058\,$mm and simulation neither helps nor hurts dense error. The sensor-A physical renderer, calibrated to a different sensor, still helps at $\Kreal=25$ ($0.125$) but less. Rotation is not improved by either renderer and is degraded at $\Kreal=100$ ($7.5^\circ\!\to\!9$--$10^\circ$), as on sensor A. The transition is earlier on sensor B ($\Kreal\approx25$ vs.\ $50$), plausibly because its uniform $1\,$mm indentation and larger contact regions make the depth-to-image mapping easier to learn from few pairs.

Table~\ref{tab:cross} evaluates the sensor-A models on the second capture. Dense error is $3$--$4\times$ the same-session value for every model, partly because the reference is limited by re-estimated poses; the informative quantity is the ordering. Contact IoU decreases monotonically with the amount of session-1 real data: $0.685$ for the simulation-only model, $0.60$--$0.65$ at $\Kreal=25$, $0.56$--$0.57$ at $\Kreal=100$, $0.54$ with all real frames; on all $1{,}109$ session-2 contact frames a reference-free ridge-mask IoU drops from session 1 to session 2 by $0.014$ (simulation only), $0.022$ ($\Kreal=25$), $0.047$ ($\Kreal=100$) and $0.055$ (all real), the same monotone trend. More session-1 real data means tighter fit to that session's appearance and worse localization in the next; simulation does not reverse this. Pose behaves oppositely: for the full-data models, adding simulation lowers raw rotation error from $15.0^\circ$ to $7.7$--$9.0^\circ$, and $\approx\!2/3$ of the remaining error is a per-object constant (Rot.$^{\ast}$ $4$--$6^\circ$ for every model), i.e.\ the re-placement offset rather than a perception error. Simulation thus acts as a session-invariant prior for pose, while dense localization across sessions remains an open problem for both real data and simulation.

\subsection{Q5: Leakage, a Fidelity Proxy, and Negative Results}
\label{sec:fidelity}

\noindent\textbf{Cost of leakage.} Across Tables~\ref{tab:loo} and \ref{tab:kshot} the leaky renderer reports depth MSE reductions of $24$--$31\%$ at every budget and on unseen objects; the strict numbers are $0$--$3\%$ for $\Kreal\le25$, $10$--$16\%$ at $\Kreal\ge50$ and $5$--$13\%$ on unseen objects. The bias is largest where the pipeline is most attractive---few or no real contacts of the target---because the leaky renderer has memorized the test contacts (it reproduces validation frames with a contact-region L1 of $0.057$, half that of any strict renderer).

\noindent\textbf{Which factor limits few-shot fidelity?}
\label{sec:negative}
Table~\ref{tab:variants} tests the hypotheses.
\emph{(i) Missing physical prior.} Pretraining the GAN on $30$k Blender pairs, conditioning it on the Blender image of the same contact, predicting a residual on top of that image, or conditioning on Blender image and background all leave contact-region fidelity unchanged or worse at $\Kreal\in\{10,25\}$ (the physical renderer's random gel-warp of $\pm3$ pixels means the conditioning image is not pixel-aligned with the target, which may limit this family).
\emph{(ii) Session illumination drift.} Session backgrounds differ by $0.05$--$0.10$ L1, more than the best renderer's total error ($0.03$); normalizing frames to a canonical background or predicting difference images improves full-image L1 by $13$--$21\%$ but leaves contact-region L1 and contact MAE unchanged (the frozen encoder is robust to the illumination field). Conditioning the renderer on the background does help on unseen objects (Sec.~\ref{sec:loo}) because it lets a renderer that never saw the object reproduce its session's illumination.
\emph{(iii) Training-side changes.} A contact-mask head with a gradient-matching loss, Blender pretraining followed by real fine-tuning, and fine-tuning with an L2-SP anchor change $\Kreal=25$ contact MAE by at most $\pm0.02\,$mm.
\emph{(iv) Contact shading.} What remains is the appearance \emph{inside} the contact (Fig.~\ref{fig:renders}), and every result is consistent with it being the limit: strict renderers separate from real-only training only after enough contacts, leakage helps exactly because it exposes the test contacts, and the residual errors are localization rather than depth-magnitude errors.
