\subsection{Problem Statement}
A tactile renderer maps a mesh $M$ and contact state $q$ (planar pose and indentation) to an image and dense labels, $(\hat I, D, N) = \mathcal{R}_\theta(M,q)$. The geometry stage $(M,q)\mapsto(D,N)$ is shared by every renderer we study; renderers differ only in the appearance operator $\mathcal{P}_\theta:(D,N)\mapsto\hat I$ and in the real data $\mathcal{D}^{r}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ used to fit $\theta$. A downstream model $f_\phi$ predicts depth, normals and planar pose from a real image. We measure a renderer by the real test error of $f_\phi$ trained on its synthetic data plus a real subset $\mathcal{D}^{r}_{\mathrm{task}}$, and we control the total real budget $\mathcal{D}^{r}_{\mathrm{cal}}\cup\mathcal{D}^{r}_{\mathrm{task}}$.

\subsection{Sensors and Data}
\noindent\textbf{Sensor A.} \sensor{} is a GelSlim-type fingertip sensor with a $17.5\,$mm square gel view imaged under six colored LED emitters. Images are center-cropped to $0.816$ of the view and resized to $224\times224$.

\noindent\textbf{Real data (session 1).} A KUKA LBR Med arm presses the sensor onto 12 ridge-pattern objects of known geometry, recording the image and end-effector pose at every contact; depth and normal references are the mesh projected at the recorded pose (geometric targets, not membrane measurements). After removing frames whose image contact region disagrees with the reference, $4{,}669$ frames remain ($222$--$568$ per object), one session per object, every tenth frame held out as validation ($472$ frames).

\noindent\textbf{Real data (session 2).} The same objects were re-placed and re-captured on another day (48 runs). Reference poses were re-estimated per frame by maximizing the IoU between the image ridge mask and the projected mesh contact and manually reviewed; we keep a curated test set of $68$ frames over $11$ objects, and use all $1{,}109$ contact frames only for a reference-free metric (Sec.~\ref{sec:cross}).

\noindent\textbf{Sensor B.} \sensorB{}~\cite{sipos2024gelslim4} is the previous generation of the same fingertip family, with a marker array, a different illumination layout and a $12\,$mm field of view. Its public depth dataset~\cite{sipos2024gelslim4} has the same 12 objects pressed by a parallel gripper at $1\,$mm indentation with mesh-derived depth and in-hand pose labels ($\approx\!1{,}000$ training and $135$ test frames per object). We use the left finger, the central $12\times12\,$mm, and report rotation after removing a per-object constant offset because its pose origin differs from ours.

\noindent\textbf{Contact sampler.} For each object we sample contact cells over the mesh at 12 base rotations ($30^\circ$ apart), an indentation of $0.8$--$1.2\,$mm and in-plane jitter, and export $D$ from the object z-buffer and $N$ by finite differences. Simulated poses are restricted to each object's real rotation and translation ranges. The same $5{,}345$ simulated contacts feed every renderer.

\subsection{Physically Calibrated Renderer}
\label{sec:blender}
The physical renderer is implemented in Blender/Cycles: a gel surface mesh is shrink-wrapped onto the object and smoothed (35 corrective iterations), shaded with a glossy/transparent material and a height-driven contact-darkening term, lit by six area emitters, and post-processed (saturation, brightness, contrast, haze, a low-frequency random gel warp). Emitter and post-processing parameters (16 scalars) are fitted by tree-based Bayesian optimization of an $L_2$ plus histogram distance between the real and rendered no-contact image (500 evaluations); gel and contact parameters were hand-tuned on a few contact frames. The real budget of this renderer is \emph{one no-contact frame}; it never sees a labeled contact.

\subsection{Learned Renderer}
\label{sec:gan}
The learned renderer replaces $\mathcal{P}_\theta$ by a conditional GAN~\cite{isola2017pix2pix,church2022tactile}: a five-level U-Net that takes the depth map and two coordinate channels and outputs an RGB image, a $70\times70$ spectral-normalized PatchGAN discriminator, and an adversarial $+\,100\,(\mathrm{L1} + \mathrm{gradient\;L1} + \mathrm{low\text{-}frequency\;L1})$ loss. Simulated depth is jittered by $\pm15\%$ at generation time.

\noindent\textbf{Real budget.} GAN-$\Kreal$ is trained on $\Kreal$ real (depth, image) pairs per object, an evenly spaced subset of each object's training frames that is \emph{the same} $\Kreal$ frames the downstream model receives; GAN-LOO on all training frames of the 9 non-held-out objects; GAN-full on all $4{,}197$ pairs. Small budgets train for at least $30$k generator updates.

\noindent\textbf{Variants.} \emph{Background-conditioned}: the session's no-contact image is appended as three input channels, so a new session costs one background frame, as for the physical renderer. \emph{Physics-conditioned}: the physical renderer's image of the same contact is appended, optionally predicting only a residual on top of it, optionally with the background. \emph{Difference image}: the target is the image minus the session background. \emph{Blender-pretrained}: pretraining on $30$k (depth, Blender image) pairs, then fine-tuning on the real pairs. \emph{Rotation coverage} changes the geometry stage instead: we vary how many of an object's real rotations appear in simulation (1, 2 or all 12) and, for the learned renderer, rotate the depth map before rendering so that every rotation is covered.

\subsection{Downstream Tactile Model}
The downstream model is identical for every renderer: a frozen sensor-invariant encoder (SITR-B/18~\cite{gupta2025sitr}) provides patch tokens at four depths; a DPT decoder~\cite{ranftl2021dpt} predicts depth and normals at $224\times224$; a pose head regresses $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,t_x,t_y)$ from the class token and mean patch token. Only decoder, pose head and an object embedding are trained ($12$M parameters). Losses are MSE on depth and normals, $1-\cos(\theta-\hat\theta)$ on rotation and L1 on translation, balanced by grouped uncertainty weighting~\cite{kendall2018multi}. Training co-mixes simulated and real frames; simulated frames receive photometric augmentation and $\pm15^\circ$ gel-spin rotation with matched labels, real frames none. AdamW, learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, batch 32, cosine schedule, 150 epochs. Depth and normal metrics use the checkpoint with the lowest validation depth MSE, pose metrics the one with the lowest validation angular error, both selected on the seen-object validation split.

\subsection{Leakage-Controlled Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
One rule: \emph{no frame used to evaluate $f_\phi$ is ever seen by the renderer.}

\noindent\textbf{Leave-object-out (LOO).} Three objects (a two-line, a rod and a cross pattern; $1{,}244$ frames) are removed from all real training, never used to calibrate a renderer, and evaluated in full. Simulated contacts of the three meshes are available to every renderer (``new object with a CAD model but no real touches''). A \emph{geometry control} additionally removes the three meshes from simulation.

\noindent\textbf{$\Kreal$-shot.} Every object contributes $\Kreal\in\{0,10,25,50,100\}$ evenly spaced real training frames. The learned renderer is trained on exactly those frames; the downstream model co-trains on them plus simulated data, or on them alone with the same number of updates. Evaluation is on the real validation split.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-session and cross-sensor.} Session-2 and sensor-B test frames are never used to train or select anything in the sensor-A study. In the sensor-B study the $\Kreal$-shot protocol is repeated with sensor-B real pairs and the sensor-B test split.

\noindent\textbf{Leaky reference.} To quantify the bias of common practice we also report GAN-full in every protocol. In LOO its training set contains the held-out objects; in $\Kreal$-shot it contains frames adjacent to the validation frames. Leaky rows are italic and never bold.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.} Depth MSE over all pixels ($\mathrm{mm}^2$), the metric of prior work on this sensor; and, because $\approx90\%$ of pixels are background, the mean absolute error inside the reference contact region (contact MAE, mm) and the contact IoU (predicted depth $>0.05\,$mm vs.\ reference $>0$), which also penalizes false contact. Normals: mean angular error inside the contact region (deg). Pose: mean angular error (deg) and normalized translation L1.
