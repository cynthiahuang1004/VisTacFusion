\section{Real-to-Sim-to-Real Pipeline}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Statement}
A tactile renderer maps a mesh $M$ and contact state $q$ (planar pose and indentation) to an image and dense labels, $(\hat I, D, N) = \mathcal{R}_\theta(M,q)$. The geometry stage $(M,q)\mapsto(D,N)$ is shared by every renderer we study; renderers differ only in the appearance operator $\mathcal{P}_\theta:(D,N)\mapsto\hat I$ and in the real data $\mathcal{D}^{r}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ used to fit $\theta$. A downstream model $f_\phi$ predicts depth, normals and planar pose from a real image. We measure a renderer by the real test error of $f_\phi$ trained on its synthetic data plus a real subset $\mathcal{D}^{r}_{\mathrm{task}}$, and we control the total real budget $\mathcal{D}^{r}_{\mathrm{cal}}\cup\mathcal{D}^{r}_{\mathrm{task}}$.

\subsection{Sensors and Data}
\noindent\textbf{Sensor A.} \sensor{} is a GelSlim-type fingertip sensor with a $17.5\,$mm square gel view imaged under six colored LED emitters. Images are center-cropped to $0.816$ of the view and resized to $224\times224$.

\noindent\textbf{Real data (session 1).} A KUKA LBR Med arm presses the sensor onto 12 ridge-pattern objects of known geometry, recording the image and end-effector pose at every contact; depth and normal references are the mesh projected at the recorded pose (geometric targets, not membrane measurements). After removing frames whose image contact region disagrees with the reference, $4{,}669$ frames remain ($222$--$568$ per object), one session per object, every tenth frame held out as validation ($472$ frames).

\noindent\textbf{Real data (session 2).} The same objects were re-placed and re-captured on another day (48 runs). Reference poses were re-estimated per frame by maximizing the IoU between the image ridge mask and the projected mesh contact and manually reviewed; we keep a curated test set of $68$ frames over $11$ objects (Sec.~\ref{sec:cross}).

\noindent\textbf{Sensor B.} \sensorB{}~\cite{sipos2024gelslim4} is the previous generation of the same fingertip family, with a marker array, a different illumination layout and a $12\,$mm field of view. Its public depth dataset~\cite{sipos2024gelslim4} has the same 12 objects pressed by a parallel gripper at $1\,$mm indentation with mesh-derived depth and in-hand pose labels ($\approx\!1{,}000$ training and $135$ test frames per object). We use the left finger, the central $12\times12\,$mm, and report rotation after removing a per-object constant offset because its pose origin differs from ours.

\noindent\textbf{Contact sampler.} For each object we sample contact cells over the mesh at 12 base rotations ($30^\circ$ apart), an indentation of $0.8$--$1.2\,$mm and in-plane jitter, and export $D$ from the object z-buffer and $N$ by finite differences. Simulated poses are restricted to each object's real rotation and translation ranges. The same $5{,}345$ simulated contacts feed every renderer.

\subsection{Physically Calibrated Renderer}
\label{sec:blender}
The physical renderer is implemented in Blender/Cycles: a gel surface mesh is shrink-wrapped onto the object and smoothed (35 corrective iterations), shaded with a glossy/transparent material and a height-driven contact-darkening term, lit by six area emitters, and post-processed (saturation, brightness, contrast, haze, a low-frequency random gel warp). Emitter and post-processing parameters (16 scalars) are fitted by tree-based Bayesian optimization of an $L_2$ plus histogram distance between the real and rendered no-contact image (500 evaluations); gel and contact parameters were hand-tuned on a few contact frames. The real budget of this renderer is \emph{one no-contact frame}; it never sees a labeled contact.

\noindent\textbf{Domain-randomization baseline.} To test whether careful calibration is necessary, we also render with randomized sensor parameters: random HSV colors and strengths for each LED emitter, random gel optics, per session, with the geometry stage (scale, field of view, smoothness) fixed. The real budget is \emph{zero}; neither a background frame nor any labeled contact is used.

\subsection{Learned Renderer}
\label{sec:gan}
The learned renderer replaces $\mathcal{P}_\theta$ by a conditional GAN~\cite{isola2017pix2pix,church2022tactile}: a five-level U-Net that takes the depth map and two coordinate channels and outputs an RGB image, a $70\times70$ spectral-normalized PatchGAN discriminator, and an adversarial $+\,100\,(\mathrm{L1} + \mathrm{gradient\;L1} + \mathrm{low\text{-}frequency\;L1})$ loss. Simulated depth is jittered by $\pm15\%$ at generation time.

\noindent\textbf{Real budget.} GAN-$\Kreal$ is trained on $\Kreal$ real (depth, image) pairs per object, an evenly spaced subset of each object's training frames that is \emph{the same} $\Kreal$ frames the downstream model receives; GAN-LOO on all training frames of the 9 non-held-out objects; GAN-full on all $4{,}197$ pairs. Small budgets train for at least $30$k generator updates.

\noindent\textbf{Variants.} \emph{Background-conditioned}: the session's no-contact image is appended as three input channels, so a new session costs one background frame, as for the physical renderer. \emph{Physics-conditioned}: the physical renderer's image of the same contact is appended, optionally predicting only a residual on top of it, optionally with the background. \emph{Difference image}: the target is the image minus the session background. \emph{Blender-pretrained}: pretraining on $30$k (depth, Blender image) pairs, then fine-tuning on the real pairs. \emph{Multi-sensor}: the background-conditioned GAN is trained on 100 simulated sensor designs (random LED layouts and gel materials) plus optionally the target sensor's real pairs; this tests whether sensor diversity can replace sensor-specific real data. \emph{Rotation coverage} changes the geometry stage instead: we vary how many of an object's real rotations appear in simulation (1, 2 or all 12) and, for the learned renderer, rotate the depth map before rendering so that every rotation is covered.

\label{sec:ppb}

The $\Kreal$-shot curves of Sec.~\ref{sec:kshot} say that the learned renderer needs $\Kreal\gtrsim50$ labeled pairs, and that what it learns from them is the appearance of real contacts. Unlabeled real frames contain exactly that appearance and cost nothing to capture (press the sensor anywhere, record nothing). We therefore propose to \emph{bootstrap} labeled pairs from unlabeled frames (Fig.~\ref{fig:ppb}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Label.} The $\Kreal$-shot model $f_0$ (trained on the $\Kreal$ real frames and GAN-$\Kreal$ data) predicts depth and planar pose on every unlabeled training frame (depth from the checkpoint with the best validation depth, pose from the one with the best validation pose). The $\Kreal$ labeled frames keep their true labels.
    \item \textbf{Align.} The teacher's depth is as blurred and as mislocalized as the teacher, but the object mesh is known, so the true label is the mesh depth at \emph{some} planar pose $(\theta,x,y)$ and indentation $z$; with the true pose the mesh label has a contact IoU of $0.95$ against the reference. We learn to find that pose with a residual aligner in the spirit of iterative render-and-compare pose refinement~\cite{li2018deepim,wang2020self6d}: a ResNet-18 takes the real frame and the mesh depth at a candidate pose as a fourth channel and regresses the correction $(\Delta\theta,\Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta z)$ that brings the candidate onto the frame. It needs no label beyond the $\Kreal$ frames: each labeled frame is paired with its true pose perturbed by up to $\pm8^\circ$, $\pm1.5\,$mm and $\pm0.15\,$mm (half of the samples at $0.3\times$ that range, for fine corrections), and the perturbation is the regression target; we add frames rendered by GAN-$\Kreal$ from the mesh at random poses within each object's labeled pose range (8k steps, batch 64, smooth-L1 loss, AdamW, $3\times10^{-4}$). At labeling time the correction is applied six times, starting from the teacher pose with $z$ the maximum of the teacher depth. Every unlabeled frame then receives the mesh depth at its aligned pose, and that pose, as its label; no frame is filtered.
    \item \textbf{Self-trained renderer.} GAN-$\Kreal$ is retrained on the $\Kreal$ true plus the aligned pairs (30k updates) and re-renders the simulated contacts.
    \item \textbf{Self-trained model.} $f_1$ is trained on the $\Kreal$ labeled frames, the aligned frames and the synthetic data, with depth, normal and pose supervision on all of them.
\end{enumerate}
PPB adds no labeled data and no change to the downstream architecture; it charges the budget one background frame per session, the unlabeled frames and the meshes that simulation already uses, and it is evaluated under the same leakage rule (validation and test frames are never pseudo-labeled, and the aligner never sees them). Where the frames are not registered to a mesh (sensor B in this study) step~2 is skipped (``PPB without alignment''): the teacher depth is the label, predicted on five windows (center and four corners of the $0.816$ crop) and stitched to the full frame; a frame is kept when the predicted contact ($>0.05\,$mm) overlaps the frame's own ridge mask (morphological black-hat; IoU $\ge0.2$; skipped on sensor B, whose marker array defeats the mask) and its indentation is plausible ($0.2$--$1.6\,$mm); and the pose loss (with its uncertainty weight) is restricted to the $\Kreal$ labeled frames. Sec.~\ref{sec:unlabeled} ablates the components: renderer only, model only, both, the alignment step and its alternatives, window stitching, and rounds.

\subsection{Zero-Label Alternative: Contact-Preserving Unpaired Refiner}
\label{sec:refiner}
PPB needs $\Kreal$ labeled pairs to start. A renderer that needs \emph{no labeled pair at all} is conceivable: geometry transfers through the contact silhouette, which the physical renderer already provides, and what is missing is the shading inside the contact, which might be learned from unlabeled real frames alone, as in unpaired image translation~\cite{shrivastava2017simgan,park2020cut,chen2022cyclegan}. We test this alternative. The refiner $\mathcal{F}$ takes the physical render $\hat I_{\mathrm{phys}}$, the session background $B$ and the contact mask $m$ (simulated depth $>0$, dilated by 4\,px and feathered) and outputs
\begin{equation}
    \hat I = m \odot \mathcal{F}(\hat I_{\mathrm{phys}}, B, m) + (1-m) \odot B ,
\end{equation}
so the background is the real one and the contact region is the only learned part; depth, normal and pose labels are those of the physical render by construction. $\mathcal{F}$ is the same U-Net as the learned renderer with a 7-channel input. It is trained against a $70\times70$ PatchGAN on \emph{unlabeled} real frames (image only; no depth, no pose) with an adversarial loss and a patchwise contrastive loss (PatchNCE~\cite{park2020cut}) between the encoder features of input and output, sampled preferentially inside $m$, which keeps the ridge structure of the render while its shading is translated; 10k generator updates, batch 8, Adam ($2\times10^{-4}$, $\beta_1=0.5$). Its real budget is the set of unlabeled frames plus one background frame per session, and the same leakage rule applies: in leave-object-out the held-out objects' frames are excluded, in $\Kreal$-shot the same $\Kreal$ frames as the downstream model are used without their labels. Applied to the domain-randomized render, the pipeline uses no calibration and no label at all.

\subsection{Downstream Tactile Model}
The downstream model is identical for every renderer: a frozen sensor-invariant encoder (SITR-B/18~\cite{gupta2025sitr}) provides patch tokens at four depths; a DPT decoder~\cite{ranftl2021dpt} predicts depth and normals at $224\times224$; a pose head regresses $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,t_x,t_y)$ from the class token and mean patch token. For the classification study, we add a 12-class head (LayerNorm on concatenated class and mean patch tokens, two-layer MLP with GELU) and train with cross-entropy alongside the other losses. Only decoder, pose head and an object embedding are trained ($12$M parameters). Losses are MSE on depth and normals, $1-\cos(\theta-\hat\theta)$ on rotation and L1 on translation, balanced by grouped uncertainty weighting~\cite{kendall2018multi}. Training co-mixes simulated and real frames; simulated frames receive photometric augmentation and $\pm15^\circ$ gel-spin rotation with matched labels, real frames none. AdamW, learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, batch 32, cosine schedule, 150 epochs. Depth and normal metrics use the checkpoint with the lowest validation depth MSE, pose metrics the one with the lowest validation angular error, both selected on the seen-object validation split.

\subsection{Leakage-Controlled Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
One rule: \emph{no frame used to evaluate $f_\phi$ is ever seen by the renderer.}

\noindent\textbf{Leave-object-out (LOO).} Three objects (a two-line, a rod and a cross pattern; $1{,}244$ frames) are removed from all real training, never used to calibrate a renderer, and evaluated in full. Simulated contacts of the three meshes are available to every renderer (``new object with a CAD model but no real touches''). A \emph{geometry control} additionally removes the three meshes from simulation.

\noindent\textbf{$\Kreal$-shot.} Every object contributes $\Kreal\in\{0,10,25,50,100\}$ evenly spaced real training frames. The learned renderer is trained on exactly those frames; the downstream model co-trains on them plus simulated data, or on them alone with the same number of updates. Evaluation is on the real validation split.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-session and cross-sensor.} Session-2 and sensor-B test frames are never used to train or select anything in the sensor-A study. In the sensor-B study the $\Kreal$-shot protocol is repeated with sensor-B real pairs and the sensor-B test split.

\noindent\textbf{Leaky reference.} To quantify the bias of common practice we also report GAN-full in every protocol. In LOO its training set contains the held-out objects; in $\Kreal$-shot it contains frames adjacent to the validation frames. Leaky rows are italic and never bold.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.} Depth MSE over all pixels ($\mathrm{mm}^2$), the metric of prior work on this sensor; and, because $\approx90\%$ of pixels are background, the mean absolute error inside the reference contact region (contact MAE, mm) and the contact IoU (predicted depth $>0.05\,$mm vs.\ reference $>0$), which also penalizes false contact. Normals: mean angular error inside the contact region (deg). Pose: mean angular error (deg) and normalized translation L1.
