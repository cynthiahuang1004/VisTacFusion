\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Tactile simulation and transfer.}
Tactile simulators such as TACTO~\cite{wang2022tacto}, Taxim~\cite{si2022taxim} and Tacchi~\cite{chen2023tacchi}, and physics-based~\cite{agarwal2021simulation}, differentiable~\cite{xu2022efficient} and GPU-based~\cite{akinola2025tacsl} variants, trade fidelity for efficiency. Image translation reduces the simulation--real gap~\cite{chen2022cyclegan,church2022tactile,zhong2025tactgen} but is trained on real images of the target sensor; our renderer study counts this supervision in the label budget. Pretrained tactile encoders~\cite{gupta2025sitr,zhao2024transferable,higuera2024sparsh} are complementary: we use the same frozen SITR encoder for all methods and vary only the supervision.

\noindent\textbf{Pseudo-labeling and pose refinement.}
Self-training labels additional data with model predictions~\cite{lee2013pseudo,xie2020noisy} and can reinforce teacher errors~\cite{arazo2020pseudo}. Known geometry constrains these labels: DeepIM~\cite{li2018deepim} learns iterative pose corrections from observed and rendered images, and Self6D~\cite{wang2020self6d} supervises pose on unlabeled RGB-D images through differentiable rendering. MeshTac learns corrections from tactile RGB and projected mesh depth and converts the refined state into dense geometry and pose targets. Unlike tactile simulation and self-training, it requires neither photorealistic rendering nor additional real labels to generate dense geometric supervision.
