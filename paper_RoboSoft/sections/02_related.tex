\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Tactile simulation and transfer.}
TACTO~\cite{wang2022tacto} renders optical tactile images, Taxim~\cite{si2022taxim} models optical response and marker motion from calibration examples, and Tacchi~\cite{chen2023tacchi} models elastomer deformation; physics-based~\cite{agarwal2021simulation}, differentiable~\cite{xu2022efficient} and GPU-based~\cite{akinola2025tacsl} simulators trade fidelity for efficiency. Image translation reduces the simulation--real gap~\cite{chen2022cyclegan,church2022tactile,zhong2025tactgen} but is trained on real images of the target sensor; our renderer study counts this supervision in the label budget. Pretrained tactile encoders~\cite{gupta2025sitr,zhao2024transferable,higuera2024sparsh} are complementary: we use the same frozen SITR encoder for all methods and vary only the supervision.

\noindent\textbf{Pseudo-labeling and pose refinement.}
Self-training labels additional data with model predictions~\cite{lee2013pseudo,xie2020noisy} and can reinforce teacher errors~\cite{arazo2020pseudo}. Known geometry constrains these labels: DeepIM~\cite{li2018deepim} learns iterative pose corrections from observed and rendered images, and Self6D~\cite{wang2020self6d} supervises pose on unlabeled RGB-D images through differentiable rendering. MeshTac learns corrections from tactile RGB and projected mesh depth and converts the refined state into dense geometry and pose targets, requiring neither photorealistic rendering nor differentiation through a renderer.
