\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Tactile simulation and transfer.}
TACTO~\cite{wang2022tacto}, Taxim~\cite{si2022taxim}, Tacchi~\cite{chen2023tacchi} and physics-based rendering~\cite{agarwal2021simulation} model tactile images through optical response and contact geometry or deformation. Differentiable~\cite{xu2022efficient} and GPU-based~\cite{akinola2025tacsl} simulation support learning and control. Transfer methods translate tactile appearance~\cite{chen2022cyclegan}, map real images to simulated depth~\cite{church2022tactile}, or generate tactile images from visual RGB-D~\cite{zhong2025tactgen}. AlignTac instead aligns mesh-projected geometry with real observations to generate supervision. We evaluate depth-to-RGB augmentation separately and count the real pairs used to fit its renderer in the label budget.

\noindent\textbf{Tactile geometry reconstruction.}
Prior work learns local contact shape or elastomer deformation directly from tactile images~\cite{bauza2019tactile,zhu2022volumetric}; AlignTac reduces the geometric labels needed to train such predictors by aligning known geometry with unlabeled real contacts.

\noindent\textbf{Tactile representations.}
Pretrained encoders such as SITR, T3 and Sparsh~\cite{gupta2025sitr,zhao2024transferable,higuera2024sparsh} reduce feature learning from scratch; all our predictors share a frozen SITR backbone. AlignTac builds on these pretrained features by providing geometric supervision for downstream prediction.

\noindent\textbf{Pseudo-labeling and pose refinement.}
Self-training uses model predictions as labels~\cite{lee2013pseudo,xie2020noisy} and can reinforce teacher errors~\cite{arazo2020pseudo}. Known geometry offers an additional constraint. DeepIM~\cite{li2018deepim} learns iterative pose corrections from observed and rendered images; Self6D~\cite{wang2020self6d} uses differentiable rendering to supervise pose on unlabeled RGB-D images. Tac2Pose~\cite{bauza2023tac2pose} estimates pose from a single touch by matching the observed contact shape to simulated contacts of a known mesh. AlignTac learns corrections from real tactile RGB and candidate mesh depth using a few labeled contacts. The learned corrections generate dense geometry and pose supervision for unlabeled images and also support optional mesh-assisted pose refinement at inference.
