\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Vision-based tactile sensing.}
GelSight-type sensors convert elastomer deformation into images under structured illumination~\cite{johnson2009retrographic,yuan2017gelsight}; GelSlim~\cite{donlon2018gelslim,sipos2024gelslim4} and DIGIT~\cite{lambeta2020digit} adapt the principle to fingertips. Because the measurement depends jointly on gel mechanics, LED layout and camera response, models trained for one sensor transfer poorly to another; sensor-invariant representations~\cite{gupta2025sitr,zhao2024transferable,higuera2024sparsh,higuera2025sparshx} and deformation-space translation~\cite{zai2025across,yang2025flow} attack the problem on the representation side. We use a frozen sensor-invariant encoder~\cite{gupta2025sitr} as the downstream backbone precisely so that our comparison isolates the data, not the representation.

\noindent\textbf{Tactile simulation.}
Physically based tactile renderers synthesize GelSight images from mesh contacts~\cite{agarwal2021simulation,wang2022tacto,luu2023simulation}, with example-based~\cite{si2022taxim}, particle-based~\cite{chen2023tacchi}, differentiable~\cite{xu2022efficient} and GPU-scale~\cite{nguyen2024tacex,akinola2025tacsl} variants. Learned renderers instead map simulated depth or contact state to sensor images with an image-to-image network~\cite{isola2017pix2pix,church2022tactile,zhong2025tactgen}. Our physically calibrated renderer follows the first line (Cycles light transport with Bayesian-optimized illumination), our learned renderer the second (pix2pix on real pairs); both share the same meshes, contact sampler and label export, so they differ only in the appearance model.

\noindent\textbf{Sim-to-real transfer and its evaluation.}
Domain randomization~\cite{tobin2017domain,peng2018sim} and structured randomization around a calibrated center~\cite{chen2024general} trade fidelity for robustness; sensor-background conditioning~\cite{zhong2025tactgen} and low-fidelity pretraining~\cite{gano2025lowfidelity} report gains from synthetic tactile data. These works measure the benefit of simulation but rarely report how much real data the simulator consumed, or whether the simulator saw the test distribution. Our contribution is the evaluation protocol---renderer and downstream model trained on the same disjoint real subset---and the resulting data-efficiency curves for both renderer families.

\noindent\textbf{Inverse rendering and system identification.}
Estimating illumination, material and camera parameters from images~\cite{kajiya1986rendering,ramamoorthi2001signal,marschner2000image} and identifying latent physical parameters from observations~\cite{ljung1999system} are the classical tools behind renderer calibration. We do not claim unique recovery of the sensor's physical parameters; the calibrated parameter vector is one member of a task-sufficient equivalence class, and we evaluate it by downstream utility.
