\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Tactile simulation and transfer.}
TACTO~\cite{wang2022tacto} renders optical tactile images, Taxim~\cite{si2022taxim} models optical response and marker motion from calibration examples, and Tacchi~\cite{chen2023tacchi} models elastomer deformation. Physics-based optical models~\cite{agarwal2021simulation}, differentiable force-field simulation~\cite{xu2022efficient} and GPU-based simulation~\cite{akinola2025tacsl} address fidelity and efficiency. Tactile image translation can reduce the simulation--real gap~\cite{chen2022cyclegan}. Church et al.~\cite{church2022tactile} translate real tactile images into simulated depth for policy transfer; TactGen~\cite{zhong2025tactgen} translates visual RGB-D into tactile images using a model trained in simulation. Our renderer study evaluates synthetic augmentation with the renderer's real supervision included in the label budget.

\noindent\textbf{Tactile representations.}
SITR~\cite{gupta2025sitr}, T3~\cite{zhao2024transferable} and Sparsh~\cite{higuera2024sparsh} learn representations that support tactile perception across sensors or tasks. MeshTac addresses the supervision used to train a downstream predictor. We use the same frozen SITR encoder for all methods to compare their training targets and data sources.

\noindent\textbf{Pseudo-labeling and pose refinement.}
Self-training uses model predictions to label additional data~\cite{lee2013pseudo,xie2020noisy}, but inaccurate predictions can reinforce teacher errors~\cite{arazo2020pseudo}. Known geometry offers a constraint on these labels. DeepIM~\cite{li2018deepim} learns iterative pose corrections from observed and rendered images, while Self6D~\cite{wang2020self6d} uses differentiable rendering to supervise pose learning on unlabeled real RGB-D images. MeshTac learns corrections from tactile RGB and projected mesh depth, then converts the refined state into dense geometry and pose targets. Label generation requires neither photorealistic appearance synthesis nor differentiation through the renderer.
