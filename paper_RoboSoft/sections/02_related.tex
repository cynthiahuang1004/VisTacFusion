\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Tactile simulation and transfer.}
TACTO~\cite{wang2022tacto} renders optical tactile images, Taxim~\cite{si2022taxim} models optical response and marker displacement from calibration examples, and Tacchi~\cite{chen2023tacchi} simulates elastomer deformation. Physics-based~\cite{agarwal2021simulation}, differentiable~\cite{xu2022efficient} and large-scale simulators~\cite{akinola2025tacsl} offer different tradeoffs between contact modeling and computational cost. Learned image translation provides another route between simulated and real tactile observations~\cite{church2022tactile,chen2022cyclegan,zhong2025tactgen}. Unpaired translation~\cite{shrivastava2017simgan,zhu2017cyclegan,park2020cut} and domain randomization~\cite{tobin2017domain} reduce dependence on paired data. Our experiments compare a physical renderer and a learned depth-to-image model under an explicit label budget. Sensor-invariant representations address variation at the feature level~\cite{gupta2025sitr,zhao2024transferable,higuera2024sparsh}; we use a frozen SITR encoder~\cite{gupta2025sitr} for all methods.

\noindent\textbf{Pseudo-labeling and pose refinement.}
Self-training uses model predictions as supervision for unlabeled data~\cite{lee2013pseudo,xie2020noisy}. Its effectiveness depends on the quality of those predictions. In visual pose estimation, DeepIM~\cite{li2018deepim} learns iterative corrections by comparing observed and rendered images, while Self6D~\cite{wang2020self6d} uses unlabeled real images for self-supervised pose learning. MeshTac adopts learned residual refinement for tactile contact states. It compares tactile RGB with mesh depth, learns corrections from perturbed labeled contacts, and uses the refined mesh to supervise dense tactile predictions. This labeling step requires neither a photorealistic renderer nor differentiation through rendering.
