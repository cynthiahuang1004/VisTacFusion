\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Tactile simulation and transfer.}
TACTO~\cite{wang2022tacto}, Taxim~\cite{si2022taxim}, Tacchi~\cite{chen2023tacchi} and physics-based rendering~\cite{agarwal2021simulation} model tactile images through optical response and contact geometry or deformation. Differentiable~\cite{xu2022efficient} and GPU-based~\cite{akinola2025tacsl} simulation support learning and control. Transfer methods translate tactile appearance~\cite{chen2022cyclegan}, map real images to simulated depth~\cite{church2022tactile}, or generate tactile images from visual RGB-D~\cite{zhong2025tactgen}. Their data requirements differ. Our study concerns depth-to-RGB renderers fitted on real contact pairs and counts those pairs in the training-label budget.

\noindent\textbf{Tactile representations.}
Pretrained encoders such as SITR, T3 and Sparsh~\cite{gupta2025sitr,zhao2024transferable,higuera2024sparsh} reduce the need to learn tactile features from scratch. MeshTac addresses the supervision for geometry and pose rather than the encoder: all predictors use the same frozen SITR backbone.

\noindent\textbf{Pseudo-labeling and pose refinement.}
Self-training uses model predictions as labels~\cite{lee2013pseudo,xie2020noisy} and can reinforce teacher errors~\cite{arazo2020pseudo}. Known geometry offers an additional constraint. DeepIM~\cite{li2018deepim} learns iterative pose corrections from observed and rendered images; Self6D~\cite{wang2020self6d} uses differentiable rendering to supervise pose on unlabeled RGB-D images. MeshTac learns corrections from tactile RGB and projected mesh depth, then uses the corrected state to supervise both dense geometry and pose on real images. Its main purpose is to improve training labels; mesh-assisted inference is optional.
