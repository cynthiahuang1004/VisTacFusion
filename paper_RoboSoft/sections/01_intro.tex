\section{Introduction}

Contact geometry tells a robot where an object meets its fingertip and how it is oriented. Vision-based tactile sensors such as GelSight, GelSlim and DIGIT~\cite{yuan2017gelsight,donlon2018gelslim,lambeta2020digit} image a deforming elastomer to capture this information. Their images vary with illumination, elastomer properties and camera response, so learning requires examples of the target sensor's appearance with accurate geometric labels. For mesh-based datasets, obtaining those labels means registering each contact to the object mesh.

Simulation reduces the need for registered real contacts by generating images and geometric labels from known meshes~\cite{wang2022tacto,si2022taxim,chen2023tacchi}. Learned renderers can bring synthetic appearance closer to the real sensor, but fitting them consumes real labels. Assessing label efficiency therefore requires counting supervision used by both the renderer and the downstream model.

Our renderer study demonstrates the consequence. Synthetic images appear substantially more useful when their renderer has access to all real training pairs. Under a shared, small training-label budget on sensor A, learned rendering slightly lowers all-pixel depth error while worsening contact error and overlap. This finding motivates using the mesh directly to supervise real images.

Unlabeled tactile images already contain the target sensor's appearance. With a known mesh, their geometric labels depend on the contact state: in-plane rotation, translation and indentation. A model trained on a small labeled set can initialize this state, but pose errors shift the projected contact and corrupt its dense labels. Accurate alignment is therefore central to making the additional images useful for learning.

MeshTac learns a residual aligner that compares the real image with candidate mesh depth and predicts a contact-state correction (Fig.~\ref{fig:mapl}). Perturbing registered contacts supplies training examples without additional real labels. The refined mesh provides depth, normal and pose targets for a student with the teacher's architecture, retaining real contact appearance while constraining the labels through geometry.

We compare real-only training, synthetic-data augmentation, self-training and MeshTac under matched training-label budgets. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Mesh-aligned pseudo-labeling that learns contact-state refinement from available labeled contacts and supplies depth, normal and pose targets for unlabeled real images.
    \item A label-accounting study separating simulation gains under matched budgets from gains obtained with additional renderer supervision.
    \item Evaluation on two sensors with separately trained models, with alignment and renderer ablations and a second capture session to test changed appearance and object placement.
    \item Test-time reuse of the aligner for pose refinement, including on held-out objects with known meshes.
\end{itemize}
