\section{Introduction}

Contact geometry tells a robot where an object meets its fingertip and how it is oriented. Vision-based tactile sensors such as GelSight, GelSlim and DIGIT~\cite{yuan2017gelsight,donlon2018gelslim,lambeta2020digit} capture contact through images of a deforming elastomer. Supervised geometry prediction requires labels paired with these sensor-specific images. For known objects, meshes can provide the labels, but each real contact must first be registered to its mesh.

Simulation generates labeled contacts from known geometry~\cite{wang2022tacto,si2022taxim,chen2023tacchi}, but its usefulness depends on how well rendered images represent the target sensor. Learned renderers can reduce this appearance gap using real image--label pairs. These pairs belong in the training-label budget; otherwise, apparent simulation gains may reflect additional real supervision.

Unlabeled real images already capture the target sensor's appearance. With a known mesh, the missing information is the contact state---in-plane rotation, translation and indentation. A teacher trained on a small registered set can estimate this state. Yet self-training with its depth predictions can preserve its errors, while projecting the mesh at an inaccurate state shifts the contact region and corrupts the dense labels.

MeshTac corrects the contact state before generating labels (Fig.~\ref{fig:mapl}). A residual aligner iteratively updates the state from the real image and candidate mesh depth. Perturbing registered contacts supplies training examples without additional real labels. The aligned mesh provides depth, normal and pose targets for a student that predicts without mesh rendering.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A mesh-aligned pseudo-labeling method that learns contact-state corrections from a small registered set and generates geometric supervision for unlabeled real images.
    \item A matched-budget evaluation protocol that includes renderer supervision in the real training-label budget, distinguishing synthetic augmentation from access to additional paired contacts.
    \item Experiments on two sensors, a second capture session and held-out objects that establish the roles of alignment and simulation, and evaluate the aligner for test-time pose refinement.
\end{itemize}
