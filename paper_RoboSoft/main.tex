\begin{abstract}
Known object meshes can provide contact geometry labels for vision-based tactile sensors, but each real image must first be registered to its mesh. We propose MeshTac, which learns from a small registered set to generate pseudo-labels for additional real images. A teacher estimates the contact state, and a residual aligner corrects it using the tactile image and projected mesh depth. The aligner is trained from perturbations of the same registered contacts, without additional real labels. The aligned mesh supplies depth, normal and planar-pose targets for a student that predicts without mesh rendering. The same aligner can optionally refine pose at inference. Our evaluation shows that, with 25 labeled contacts per object and no synthetic images, MeshTac reduces contact-region depth MAE by $21\%$ and $36\%$ on two tactile sensors relative to real-only training. On contact metrics, it matches real-only models trained with twice as many labels. Synthetic augmentation provides little further improvement within the training session but helps on a second session at the larger budget. A renderer fitted on all real training pairs reduces depth MSE by $24$--$26\%$, compared with $0$--$3\%$ under matched budgets. These results show that accurate contact-state alignment is critical for converting known meshes into effective supervision, while fair renderer comparisons must account for the real image--label pairs used to train them.
\end{abstract}
