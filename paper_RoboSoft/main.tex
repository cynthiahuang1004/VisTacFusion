\begin{abstract}
For known objects, a tactile system may have an object mesh but only a few real contacts registered to it. Simulation can supply additional image--label pairs, although a learned renderer may itself consume substantial real supervision. We introduce MeshTac, which uses a small registered set to align known meshes with unlabeled tactile images. A teacher initializes the contact state, and a residual aligner trained with perturbed labeled states corrects it. Projecting the aligned mesh provides depth, normal and pose targets for a student trained on real images. Every trainable component shares the same real training-label budget, and standard student inference requires no mesh rendering. On ridge-pattern objects, with models trained separately for two sensors, MeshTac improves contact reconstruction over real-only training, synthetic augmentation and teacher-based self-training. The improvement persists without learned rendering, showing that aligned geometry itself provides useful supervision; synthetic data adds robustness when the capture session changes. A separate label-accounting study shows that real pairs used to fit a renderer can substantially alter the apparent benefit of simulation. The aligner can also refine pose at inference when the mesh is available.
\end{abstract}
