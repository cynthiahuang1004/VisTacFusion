\begin{abstract}
Vision-based tactile sensors need dense geometric labels from the target sensor, yet registering real contacts to an object mesh is slow even when the mesh is known. Two common remedies fall short with few labels. Simulation renders image--label pairs from the mesh, but with few real pairs a learned renderer does not improve contact reconstruction, and a renderer fitted on more real pairs than the predictor overstates the value of simulation. Self-training reuses the predictor's own depth and inherits its localization errors. We observe that what unlabeled images lack is not appearance but the contact pose that places the known mesh in them. MeshTac learns this registration from the labeled set: a teacher proposes the pose, a residual aligner trained on perturbed labeled poses corrects it, and the aligned mesh yields depth, normal and pose labels for a student that predicts without the mesh. All trainable components share one real-label budget. With 25 labels per object, MeshTac lowers contact depth error by $20\%$ on an in-house sensor and $36\%$ on public GelSlim~4.0 data, matching real-only training with twice the labels, and the gain persists without any renderer; synthetic data helps when the capture session changes. A renderer fitted on all training pairs reports a $24$--$26\%$ depth-MSE reduction that falls to $0$--$3\%$ at matched budgets. The aligner also refines predicted poses at test time.
\end{abstract}
