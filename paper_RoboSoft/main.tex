\begin{abstract}
Vision-based tactile learning supports dexterous manipulation and physical interaction, but collecting accurately registered training data remains a bottleneck. We introduce AlignTac, which aligns real tactile images with simulated contact geometry to learn from few registered contacts. Our key insight is that real images already capture the sensor's appearance, while a known object mesh supplies dense geometry once its low-dimensional contact state is aligned. A teacher initializes this state, and a residual aligner corrects it using the real image and mesh-projected depth. We train the aligner by perturbing the mesh states of the same registered contacts while keeping their images fixed, without additional real labels or synthetic tactile RGB images. The aligned geometry supplies depth, normal and planar-pose pseudo-labels for a student whose standard inference requires no mesh; the same aligner optionally refines pose at inference. With 25 labeled contacts per object, AlignTac reduces contact-region depth MAE by $20\%$ and $36\%$ on two tactile sensors relative to real-only training, matching real-only contact accuracy with twice as many training labels. Under the same teacher initialization, alignment raises pseudo-label IoU from $0.47$ to $0.72$, showing that known geometry alone is insufficient. Synthetic augmentation is complementary under the tested session shift at the larger label budget, but renderer comparisons must account for the real image--label pairs used to fit them. Our code will be released to foster future research.
\end{abstract}
